How to keep track of Christmas gifts

Updated September 2026

The short answer. Pick one place and put three things in it: who you are buying for, what you have thought of for each of them, and what you have actually spent. Everything else is decoration. A notes app works, a spreadsheet works better because it adds up, and an app works best because it also remembers what you gave last year and warns you before the budget is gone.

Why the shoebox method fails every December

Most people track Christmas gifts across four places at once. A few ideas in Notes, a couple of screenshots in Photos, some things already bought and hidden in a cupboard, and a rough total in their head that is always wrong by about thirty percent.

It holds together until the middle of December, and then three things go wrong at the same time. You buy something you already bought. You forget a person entirely until Christmas Eve. And you find out what you actually spent in January, when the card statement arrives.

None of that is a discipline problem. It is a record keeping problem, and it has a boring fix.

The three things worth tracking

  1. People, not presents. Start from the list of everyone you buy for, because the person you forget is always the one who was never written down. Count them honestly, including the ones you only buy small things for.
  2. Ideas separately from purchases. An idea is not a commitment. If your list cannot tell the difference between "maybe a scarf" and "bought, in the wardrobe", you will either double buy or panic buy.
  3. A budget per occasion, not one big number. "Christmas: 500" is easy to blow through without noticing. Knowing you have 60 left for your brother is what actually changes a decision in a shop.

Set it up in ten minutes

  1. Write down every person. Do this first and do it in one sitting.
  2. Put a number next to each name. It does not need to be fair or final, it just needs to exist.
  3. Add the ideas you already have, even vague ones.
  4. Mark anything you have already bought, and what it cost.
  5. Check the total against what you actually want to spend, and adjust now rather than in December.

The part almost nobody tracks, and should

What you gave, per person, per year.

It sounds like overkill until the first time it saves you. You remember that you bought your dad something good two years ago but not what it was, so you either ask someone and ruin the surprise, or you buy him the same style of thing again. A one line record per person per year removes that problem permanently, and it quietly makes you better at gifting, because patterns show up. You can see that the small practical presents landed better than the big gestures.

This is the main reason to keep a gift list across years rather than starting fresh each December.

How much should Christmas gifts cost?

There is no correct number, and anyone quoting a percentage of your salary is guessing. The useful version of the question is narrower: decide the total you are willing to spend before you start, split it across the people on your list, and then track against it. A budget you set in November and watch in December does more work than any rule of thumb.
